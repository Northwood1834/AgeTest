# Session timing and evidence boundary

## Product rule (v1.1)

A set contains exactly 12 short tasks (normally about 4–6 minutes). Each profile may complete up to four sets in a rolling 20-hour training window.

- The window starts when the first set begins.
- Completing sets 1–3 starts a five-minute inter-set break.
- Completing set 4 blocks new sets until the original 20-hour window ends.
- If the window ends before all four sets are used, the next set starts a fresh window.
- An unfinished set remains resumable and does not consume one of the four completions until all 12 answers are finished.

This allows roughly 16–24 minutes of actual play at the maximum dose, but splits it into short blocks with recovery time. The rolling window prevents a midnight reset from granting eight closely packed sets. The schedule is per local profile.

The five-minute and 20-hour values are evidence-informed **product choices**, not medically validated optimal intervals. Published cognitive-training protocols are heterogeneous and do not establish an exact consumer-game dose or a universal minute at which “brain fatigue” begins.

## Evidence considered

- Lampit A, Hallock H, Valenzuela M. “Computerized Cognitive Training in Cognitively Healthy Older Adults: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of Effect Modifiers.” *PLoS Medicine* 11(11), 2014. DOI: [10.1371/journal.pmed.1001756](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1001756). The review found modest average effects and substantial dependence on study/design features; it does not validate this app's dose.
- Albulescu P et al. “Give me a break! A systematic review and meta-analysis on the efficacy of micro-breaks for increasing well-being and performance.” *PLoS ONE* 17(8), 2022. DOI: [10.1371/journal.pone.0272460](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0272460). Micro-breaks of up to ten minutes produced small improvements in vigor and fatigue, while overall performance effects were not statistically significant; this supports offering a break, not claiming five minutes is uniquely optimal.
- Tullo D et al. “Investigating the Role of Individual Differences in Adherence to Cognitive Training.” *Journal of Cognition* 6(1), 2023. DOI: [10.5334/joc.315](https://doi.org/10.5334/joc.315). Its review and adherence analysis describe mixed spacing findings and individual variation.
- Wang Z et al. “Training Distribution as a Key Manipulator for Spatial Updating Transfer.” *Frontiers in Human Neuroscience* 8:217, 2014. DOI: [10.3389/fnhum.2014.00217](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2014.00217). It supports investigating distributed rather than massed practice without establishing a universal daily rule.

## Interpretation

The app's limit manages entertainment pacing, fatigue, and score farming. It does **not** claim that four sets improve cognition, prevent decline, treat disease, or match a clinical program. Players should stop earlier if they repeatedly reread instructions, make unusual easy-task errors, become irritable or sleepy, or develop headache or eye strain.

The cap, break, and window can be revised from pilot completion and fatigue feedback. Any future change must be described as a product-rule change rather than newly proven medicine.
